The world is bigger, much more complex and constantly changing and evolving. This never-ending categorizing of people into good or bad, enemy or ally, pro or contra is very exhausting. Hating on people and despising them for not thinking like you is not the right way.

It does not help anyone, as hate corrodes the container it is carried in. You can have different opinions and/or not like people, and still be civilized about it. The art to "agree to disagree" seems to have vanished in times where discourse and tolerating another person's opinion is needed more than ever.

And no, this does not mean that Nazis, alt-right nut jobs, "conservatives" and all the other mongrels should be allowed to say whatever they want, twisting facts and constantly spewing hate under the mantle of "free speech", "opinions" and "beliefs". There is a fine line, especially when dealing with Nazis. But as long as people do not cross this line, their opinion needs to be tolerated. 

It is harder and harder these days to see that fine line, after far too many people trampled all over it, trying their best to move it or make it invisible. We need to be strong and vigilant to not succumb to their hateful rhetoric and to not apply their ways and methods ourselves. We need to be constantly patrolling and protecting this line – and to remember its importance. But once they cross the line, the time for indulgence and leniency is over. Fight them with all you got and give them hell. There is no place for this level of intolerance or Nazis in this world.

> Unlimited tolerance must lead to the disappearance of tolerance. If we extend unlimited tolerance even to those who are intolerant, if we are not prepared to defend a tolerant society against the onslaught of the intolerant, then the tolerant will be destroyed, and tolerance with them.—In this formulation, I do not imply, for instance, that we should always suppress the utterance of intolerant philosophies; as long as we can counter them by rational argument and keep them in check by public opinion, suppression would certainly be most unwise. But we should claim the right to suppress them if necessary even by force; for it may easily turn out that they are not prepared to meet us on the level of rational argument, but begin by denouncing all argument; they may forbid their followers to listen to rational argument, because it is deceptive, and teach them to answer arguments by the use of their fists or pistols. We should therefore claim, in the name of tolerance, the right not to tolerate the intolerant. We should claim that any movement preaching intolerance places itself outside the law and we should consider incitement to intolerance and persecution as criminal, in the same way as we should consider incitement to murder, or to kidnapping, or to the revival of the slave trade, as criminal.  
>
> — Karl Popper (1945)

My thoughts are neither new, nor original, I am well aware of that. But with all the divisiveness and hate out there, I had to write them down. So that I can read them repeatedly, again and again and again, in testing, confusing and „interesting“ times like these. As a reminder, to never become like *them*.
